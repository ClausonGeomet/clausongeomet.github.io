THE MATHEMATICAL IDEA

The most unusual core isn't always the best addition to the batch.

A geologically interesting specimen and an informative testwork programme are not necessarily the same thing.

GEOLOGICAL COVERAGE

Where are the gaps?

Different domains, spatial settings and assay compositions may need representation. Selecting more samples from the same geological neighbourhood can also create redundancy.

BAYESIAN INFORMATION GAIN

Where might we learn most?

A Gaussian-process model estimates how a proposed test could reduce uncertainty at specified prediction targets. After adding one candidate to the batch, another may contribute less because their information overlaps.

In the frozen challenge, an extremely SiO₂-rich specimen can rank highly as an individual information-gain candidate yet be omitted from the cost-aware combination. That is not a contradiction: the batch also depends on its costs, alternative specimens and information overlap.

The score is model-expected learning, not realised prediction error. One seeded Random comparison does not establish that IG is generally superior.
