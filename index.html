01 / THE APPROACH

More than modelling.
From data to decisions.

What is statistical geometallurgy? At Clauson Geomet, it means applying statistical thinking to the entire journey from geological and processing data to a decision: how data are sampled, structured, checked, analysed, interpreted and put to work.

01 / THE WHOLE WORKFLOW

Better data.
Not just better models.

Statistics does not start with fitting a model, or end with reporting one. I bring the same care to data organisation, quality control, visualisation and communication as to the analysis itself — building reproducible workflows that others can understand, check and reuse.

02 / DOMAIN-INFORMED INFERENCE

Small samples.
Careful inference.

Geometallurgical decisions often rely on limited testwork. Drawing conclusions beyond those samples requires an understanding of the orebody, the sampling and the process, alongside appropriate statistical techniques. Experience guides the questions and assumptions; it does not replace representative sampling, validation or honest uncertainty.

03 / METHODS INTO PRACTICE

Rigorous methods.
Useful tools.

I turn statistical approaches into practical, problem-specific implementations: reproducible R and Python workflows, interactive applications and decision-support tools. Where standard approaches fall short, I develop and test new implementations around the problem — with clear assumptions, documented methods and outputs people can use.

Domain knowledge, not just model fit. A model can fit the data and still answer the wrong question. The difference is knowing what the samples represent, how the process works and which conclusions the evidence can support.

The whole-workflow perspective draws inspiration from Hadley Wickham and the tidyverse community: data organisation, exploration, programming and communication are integral to analysis, not just preparation for a model. See R for Data Science and Welcome to the Tidyverse.

COMPOSITION & MINERALOGY

Compositional data analysis

Geochemical assays and mineral proportions describe parts of a material. Their relative structure matters. My research focuses on methods that respect these relationships and quantify uncertainty in mineralogical interpretation, including element-to-mineral conversion.

How can we extract geological meaning without mistaking compositional constraints for physical relationships?

CAUSE & PROCESS RESPONSE

Causal inference

When plant performance changes, did the ore change, did an intervention work, or did both happen together? My focus is on separating association from intervention effects through process knowledge, explicit causal assumptions and appropriate study design — and recognising when the available data cannot distinguish them.

What would change if we acted differently — and what evidence supports that conclusion?

06 / ABOUT

Geometallurgy

Matt Clauson is a geometallurgist and statistical modeller with more than 10 years of experience across geology, mineral processing, operating plants and applied data science.

His career has spanned resource and geometallurgical studies, plant-facing technical roles and operational analytics, giving him experience at both ends of the mine-to-mill chain. He has worked with geological, mineralogical, metallurgical and plant-operating data to understand how ore variability translates into changes in throughput, recovery, product quality, energy use and processing risk.

His work includes geometallurgical modelling, mine-to-mill troubleshooting, predictive domaining, metallurgical testwork analysis, mineralogical characterisation, process forecasting, causal inference, uncertainty analysis and development of reproducible analytical tools in R and Python.

His research interests centre on compositional data analysis and causal inference. Through Clauson Geomet, he brings domain experience and statistical reasoning together, developing practical implementations that connect orebody knowledge, testwork and plant data to decisions.

Matt holds an MSc in Mining Geology, a BSc (Hons) in Geology and a Graduate Diploma of Science in Mathematics & Statistics.
